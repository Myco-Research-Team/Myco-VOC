# Myco-VOC
Plants emit V olatile Organic Compounds both ambiently & in response to both biotic and
abiotic stresses. VOC emissions in plants can play important roles in plant resistance against
biotic stressors such as herbivory& pathogens, trophic interactions with pollinators & plant
symbionts in both above and below-ground environments, and on a larger scale, tropospheric
interactions such as cloud seeding, Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi ( AMF) form close symbiotic
associations w/ plants, penetrating the cell walls of the plant’s root cells to form arbuscules
where nutrient-carbon exchange happens. AMF changes the plant’s metabolism indirectly by
providing increased nutrients and water in exchange for photosynthates as well as by essentially
acting as a carbon sink and affecting the plant’s photosynthetic capacity. However,, AMF have
also been shown to directly mediate antioxidant systems, change phytohormone biosynthesis,
and change the expression of certain stress-response genes. This study will investigate whether
AMF also have the capacity to change how VOC emissions from Populus trichocarpa play out
temporally in response to heat stress events. Saplings planted under zero,low, medium, & high
mycorrhizal availability will be subjected to temperatures of 35 & 45 degrees Celsius for 1 hour
long intervals to simulate short heat stress events. Th corresponding VOC emissions will be
collected, measured, & compared using GC-MS.
